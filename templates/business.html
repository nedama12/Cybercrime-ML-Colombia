{% endif %} {% call fold('Problem and context', kicker='1.1', open=true) %}

Cybercrime cases registered in Colombia grew more than nine times in ten years. Victims usually know what happened to them ("money left my account", "someone entered my WhatsApp") but not what the offense is called, what to do first or where to file a complaint. This delays the reaction (blocking cards, recovering accounts), reduces formal complaints and leaves the authorities without a description of how people are attacked: official records count cases but contain no narrative of the incident.

Approved problem: classify a citizen's short incident report into the legal category of cybercrime defined by Law 1273 of 2009, so the platform can give the right first steps, route the report to an analyst and build regional evidence of new fraud patterns.

{% endcall %} {% call fold('Questions and learning task', kicker='1.4') %}
  • Main question: which category of Law 1273 of 2009 does this report describe, and how confident is the model?
  • Secondary question (benchmark): can the category be predicted only from where and when a case was registered?
  • Learning task: supervised multiclass classification of short text plus structured fields. Target: category (8 classes: electronic theft, transfer fraud, phishing or spoofing, unauthorized access, personal data violation, malware, attack on systems, tampered mobile device).

{% endcall %} {% call fold('Data quality, relevance and sufficiency', kicker='1.6') %}
  • Official data: complete and without duplicates, but aggregated (each row groups cases) and without any description of the incident. It is relevant for context and statistics, but insufficient to classify what a victim reports.
  • Seed corpus: balanced (260 reports per category) and written from the legal definitions, so it is relevant; its limitation is that it is not real victim text. This is controlled with grouped cross-validation and an independent realistic holdout.
  • Sufficiency: enough to train a text classifier with stable cross-validation (standard deviation {{ '%.3f'|format(m.cv_f1_macro_std|default(0)) }}); real reports confirmed by analysts are the path to increase it.
{% endcall %} {% call fold('Feasibility, limitations and risks', kicker='1.7') %}

Technical feasibility: high. Scikit-learn pipelines, a model of about 0.7 MB and inference in milliseconds run on a free Render instance. Organisational feasibility: the platform needs analysts to review reports; it does not replace the official complaint channels.
