{% call fold('Conclusion', kicker='Summary', open=true) %}

The type of cybercrime cannot be inferred from where and when a case was registered, but it can be identified from a short description of what the victim experienced. The platform therefore asks citizens three simple questions, classifies the report, gives practical steps and keeps every report for analyst review. Each confirmed report becomes new training data.

{% endcall %} {% call fold('Limitations', kicker='What to keep in mind') %}
  • The report classifier was trained on a seed corpus written by the team, not on real victim reports. The realistic holdout is small ({{ report_info.holdout_rows|default(0) }} reports).
  • The official data is aggregated: each row is a group of cases, not an individual incident.
  • The categories come from an approximate mapping of legal articles, and the classes are imbalanced.
  • The platform does not replace a criminal complaint; it helps citizens prepare one.
{% endcall %} {% call fold('Next steps', kicker='Improvement plan') %}
  • Retrain monthly with analyst-confirmed reports and track agreement between model and analysts.
  • Compare regional patterns from citizen reports with the official yearly figures.
  • Validate the categories and wording with cybercrime specialists.
