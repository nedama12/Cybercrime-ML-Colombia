{% endcall %} {% call fold('How domain knowledge shaped the model', kicker='3.5') %}
  • The 8 classes follow the articles of Law 1273 of 2009 (269A-269J) and Law 1453 of 2011, art. 105, so every prediction has a legal meaning and a list of first steps.
  • Victims describe effects, not offenses ("money disappeared", "they entered my account"), so the corpus was written from the victim's point of view and in both languages used in Colombia's digital channels.
  • Phrases overlap on purpose (bank, account, message) because real cases combine offenses, e.g. phishing followed by theft; that is why the second most likely category is also shown.
  • The channel and affected assets are strong legal clues (a changed IMEI is always a device; a downed website is an attack on systems), so they are included as structured features.
  • The location benchmark (macro F1 {{ '%.2f'|format(info.metrics.f1_macro|default(0)|float) }}) showed that place and year cannot identify the offense, which is why the citizen's description became the core input.
