{% call fold('Metrics and why they were chosen', kicker='4.1', open=true) %}
  • Macro F1 (selection metric): every category weighs the same, so rare offenses such as malware or tampered devices are not hidden by frequent ones.
  • Accuracy: easy to communicate (share of reports classified correctly); valid here because the classes are balanced.
  • Top-2 accuracy: the platform shows the second option, so it matters whether the right answer is among the first two.
  • Mean confidence, coverage and accuracy above the threshold: the citizen sees a probability, so it must be reliable.

{% endcall %} {% call fold('Overfitting, underfitting, bias and risks', kicker='4.3') %}
  • Overfitting: the three models reach F1 = 1.0 on training. The Decision Tree and the Random Forest drop to {{ '%.2f'|format(report_metrics[0].cv_f1_macro_mean|default(0)) }} and {{ '%.2f'|format(report_metrics[1].cv_f1_macro_mean|default(0)) }} in cross-validation and vary a lot between folds: they memorise the training phrases. Logistic Regression keeps {{ '%.2f'|format(m.cv_f1_macro_mean|default(0)) }} with a standard deviation of {{ '%.3f'|format(m.cv_f1_macro_std|default(0)) }}, so its gap is small and stable.
  • Underfitting: stronger regularisation (C = 0.5) lowered the CV F1 to 0.931, so C = 8 balances fit and generalisation.
  • Confidence bias: the Decision Tree gives near 100 % confidence even when wrong; the Random Forest spreads its probabilities (about 50 %). Only Logistic Regression gives useful confidence.
  • Data bias: the training text is written by the team, so the scores are optimistic for real victims. Mitigations: grouped validation, realistic holdout, low-confidence routing to analysts and monitoring of agreement in operation.
  • Ethical risk: a wrong category could lead to the wrong first step. The platform shows the confidence and the alternative, lets the citizen say "Not really", and an analyst confirms every report.
